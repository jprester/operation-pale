# Creative direction — v0.1

## Format
An **interactive documentary from the future, with a navigable technical archive**. Readers can follow a directed cinematic path or explore individual chapters. The tone suggests historical investigation rather than a military recruitment interface.

## Audience feeling
A familiar world continues functioning while sovereignty changes; scenes should convey scale, procedural inevitability, uncertainty, and an unsettling absence of theatrical villainy.

## Visual system
- Dark charcoal / cold blue-black base, porcelain-white type, restrained amber timestamps and highlights.
- Large documentary stills, slow camera moves, precise annotations, sparse motion typography.
- Globe and orbital diagrams for scale; human spaces for impact; bureaucratic documents for institutional decision making.
- Avoid glowing neon sci-fi HUD overload, generic alien saucers, obvious video-game combat indicators.
- Distinguish reconstructions and hypothetical visuals from purported 'archive' evidence.

## Interaction
- Scroll/continue controls advance directed scenes.
- Optional timeline allows jumping between chapters.
- Expandable technical dossier links do not interrupt narrative flow.
- Narration optional, with text captions always available; no forced autoplay audio.

## Production strategy
Start with the 60–90 second first-contact *experience prototype* (not a finished episode): a composed planet shot, careful text transitions, and a clear CTA to the Notification. Expand only when the visual language and pace feel convincing.

## Accessibility and performance
Text remains HTML; keyboard navigation; avoid motion-dependent comprehension; reduce motion when requested; mobile fallback still works without WebGL.
