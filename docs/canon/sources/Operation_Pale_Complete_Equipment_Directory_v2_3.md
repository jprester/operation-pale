## 1. Revision Summary

Version 2.3 incorporates the following major changes:

- Wain is now **Caravan**; Seneschal is now **Legate**; Works is now the **Foundry network**, divided into Foundry Alpha, Beta, Gamma, and Delta.
- The opening Wave is no longer a thirty-hour night-following sequence designed primarily around military crew survival. It is now a **compressed global suppression operation**, with strategically important targets serviced in the first hour and bulk dismantling completed over the following several hours.
- The only hard casualty rule is the charter ceiling: **total human deaths must remain below five percent of world population**. Military casualties have no separate protected status.
- **Coffer** is formalized as a specialized orbital Quiver staging carrier rather than a generic transport ship.
- **Picket** is formalized as the disposable strategic interceptor layer supporting Aegis.
- **Stork**, **Merlin**, and rare **Roost** aerostats are added to the opening counter-air architecture.
- **Snare** is redesigned from a reusable six-dart interceptor into a one-way loitering runway interceptor with a terminal rocket dash.
- **Vulture** is added as the principal persistent anti-vehicle loitering munition.
- **Harrow** is revised as a passive, reusable regional weapons carrier, with clearer propulsion, payload, deployment, and doctrinal boundaries.
- Opening atmospheric deployment is formalized as a layered standoff architecture: Harrows form the inner strike screen; Storks form the outer interceptor-carrier screen.
- Lantern, Aegis, Shrike, Picket, Stork, Merlin, Snare, Harrow, Vulture, Coffer, and Quiver are integrated into a single H-hour order of battle.

## 2. Strategic Design Rules

### 2.1 Technology and physics

The Company fields technology roughly one century beyond 2026 humanity. Interstellar transport is the sole major handwave. Orbital mechanics, atmospheric propagation, thermal management, manufacturing, sensor geometry, communications delay, and material fatigue remain binding.

### 2.2 The charter

The Legate has six years to deliver a productive, administered Earth requiring no continuing major military commitment. The schedule is external and economically binding.

### 2.3 Casualty rule

The only hard ethical limit is total human deaths below five percent of world population. Below that ceiling, casualties are an operational variable rather than an independent moral objective.

The Company still avoids deaths when doing so:

- preserves expertise needed to operate infrastructure;
- prevents nuclear escalation or interstate misidentification;
- reduces later resistance and campaign duration;
- protects ports, grids, reactors, food systems, and industrial continuity;
- reduces Company losses or manifest expenditure.

Military personnel actively operating targeted systems receive no special protection. The Company attacks capabilities rather than pursuing professional groups as a general policy, because aircraft, fuel, command systems, spares, and weapons are usually cheaper targets than individual specialists.

### 2.4 Preservation doctrine

The Company preserves four categories because they cannot be recreated inside the charter:

1. people and practical expertise useful to a functioning planet;
2. the biosphere;
3. deep emplacement such as mines, dams, dredged channels, graded transport corridors, and cultivated land;
4. continuity of life support, especially grid, water, food, medicine, and nuclear safety.

Everything readily replaceable may be destroyed when destruction is faster or more reliable than capture.

### 2.5 Industrial split

The Foundry network can manufacture bulk structure, airframes, tanks, propellant, explosives, actuators, wiring, simple optics, and crude correlation seekers. It cannot bootstrap compact high-density fusion, adaptive-optics beam directors, high-grade imaging seekers, or dense compute inside the schedule.

The finite manifest remains the campaign's deepest constraint.

| --- | ---: | --- |
| Compact fusion cores | 322 t | Finite high-value powerplants; no casual proliferation of fusion aircraft |
| Beam directors and adaptive optics | 389 t | Aegis and Shrike losses are strategically permanent |

### Foundry Alpha — Volatiles and propellant

Produces water, reaction mass, industrial gases, chemical feedstock, and low-complexity propellants from cislunar and asteroidal resources.

### Foundry Beta — Metals and bulk structure

Produces alloy, hulls, airframes, tanks, cable, munition bodies, pressure vessels, structural trusses, and bulk machinery.

### Foundry Gamma — Integration and final assembly

Combines locally produced structures with manifest-grade components. It handles final assembly, testing, systems integration, and qualification of high-capability platforms.

### Foundry Delta — Precision maintenance and salvage

Repairs, recoats, recalibrates, harvests, and recombines shipped precision components. It extends manifest life but cannot create a semiconductor or adaptive-optics ecosystem from raw material.

### Anchor depots

Distributed cislunar logistics nodes holding reaction mass, spare structures, bulk stores, and staging hardware. Their purpose is to prevent a single-depot failure.

### Belt Reserve

A distant unmanned insurance node. It is not routine campaign logistics and cannot rapidly replace losses near Earth.

### Tugs

Slow reusable orbital workhorses that move Quivers, Coffers, Casks, raw feedstock, and structural modules between Foundries, Anchors, and operational orbits. Most cislunar mass movement is performed by tugs rather than completed self-propelled combat platforms.

Harrow is a persistent regional weapons magazine. It waits above likely movement corridors and releases the cheapest suitable munition after Lantern, Domesday, Hound, Herald, Tithing, or surface systems verify a target.

- outer reserve approximately 300–600 km from defended cores;

Typical payload cassettes:

- Scythes or Darts for known targets;
- 3–6 Vultures for moving targets;
- limited Snare or sensor packages;

## 12. Atmospheric and Orbital Deployment Doctrine

### 12.1 Pre-H-hour deployment

- Lanterns deploy first and establish weather, communications, and regional surveillance.
- Aegis and Shrike deploy late enough to reduce warning but early enough to reach fixed stations.
- Picket aerostat racks deploy in the final 72–36 hours.
- Storks enter approximately 6–18 hours before H-hour and hold outside defended cores.
- Harrows enter in the final hours, disperse into outer and sector holding belts, and remain passive.
- Snares enter or are air-deployed around priority runways and alternate strips.
- Coffers hold Quivers in staged orbital groups until final release.

### 12.2 H-hour standoff geometry

**Harrows form the inner airborne strike screen.** They remain close enough to service movement corridors and emitters but initially outside the densest SAM zones.

**Storks form the outer interceptor-carrier screen.** They remain farther away and send Merlins into the contested region.

The Quiver strike opens corridors by attacking fighter bases, radars, command systems, and long-range air defence. Harrows and Storks move inward only as the human air picture fragments.

### 12.3 Survivability principles

- external cueing rather than active radar;
- distributed, non-repeating holding patterns;
- standoff before suppression;
- no dense formations;
- acceptance of some Harrow and Snare losses;
- conservative employment of Storks and manifest-grade stratospheric assets;
- rapid reallocation by Wardens and Lanterns.

## 13. Revised Opening Wave: H+0 to H+60

The opening attack is operationally simultaneous rather than a thirty-hour warning sequence.

### H+0 to H+3 minutes — Strategic containment

- Notification begins simultaneously with terminal strikes.
- Aegis and Picket enter full launch-intercept readiness.
- Strategic bomber alert areas, exposed mobile nuclear support, launch communications, military SATCOM, and airborne command aircraft are attacked.
- Early-warning systems needed to prevent interstate misidentification are preserved where practical.

### H+3 to H+10 — Airbase suppression

- Scythes attack aircraft, fuel distribution, maintenance, radars, mission systems, and support vehicles.
- Mattocks open hardened shelters and protected command facilities.
- Shrikes engage aircraft above weather.
- Snares attack runway and climb-out geometry.
- Storks release Merlins against unexpected low-altitude survivors.

### H+10 to H+20 — Air-defence and command fragmentation

- Active radars and emitters are attacked.

## 14. Role Boundaries

### Harrow, Petrel, and Stork

- **Harrow:** persistent regional weapons carrier; waits above a region and releases effects.
- **Petrel:** local tactical VTOL; works inside a ground operation from Nests.
- **Stork:** theatre-level carrier and transport; relocates platforms and infrastructure.

### Scythe, Dart, Snare, and Vulture

- **Scythe:** known target at a known location.
- **Dart:** small confirmed local target.
- **Snare:** aircraft committed to runway or climb geometry.
- **Vulture:** moving armour, vehicles, artillery, emitters, and mobile launchers.
- **Mattock:** protected mass and hardened structure.

### Aegis, Picket, Shrike, Merlin, and Snare

- **Aegis:** boost-phase ballistic missile defence.
- **Picket:** strategic leakage and saturation interceptor.
- **Shrike:** clear upper-air engagement and sensor blinding.
- **Merlin:** unexpected low-level and bad-weather aircraft.
- **Snare:** predictable runway and climb-out interception.
