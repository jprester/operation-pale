# 0. Executive Summary

The v2.1 force is redesigned around the campaign’s actual bottlenecks: target verification, bad-weather defence, submarine search, satellite reconstruction, administrative scale, and the preservation of irreplaceable precision hardware. The Company already possesses enough destructive power to make military victory overdetermined. The purpose of this pass is to reduce waste after victory.

The main conceptual change is that drones are no longer treated as isolated specialist platforms. They form a layered machine ecology. A small number of high-capability nodes coordinate large numbers of cheap, locally manufactured scouts, interceptors, relays, crawlers, security units, and consumable effects. Human resistance mirrors this structure with less capable but highly distributed systems.

The largest force changes are: fewer Shrikes and full Monoliths; a smaller active Harrow fleet; far more reusable reconnaissance and target-verification drones; mobile sub-cloud point defence; robust chokepoint sensors; oceanic gliders; disposable cargo entry; precision repair capacity; and a procedural administrative tier below the Reeve.

## Force-design constraints

- One-century technology gap. The interstellar drive is the sole major handwave; atmospheric propagation, energy density, thermodynamics, communications delay, and manufacturing friction remain real.

- Bulk mass can be manufactured locally. Compact fusion cores, adaptive optics, high-grade recognition sensors, dense compute, and a complete precision-industrial ecosystem cannot.

- High-capability autonomy is capped at roughly 900,000 platforms. Millions of additional systems are possible only through narrow autonomy, local coordination, human review, and cheap terrestrial electronics.

- No magical cyber capability. The Company does not defeat modern cryptography or steal authentication keys; it controls access, operators, physical infrastructure, and ordinary authenticated channels.

- The Company preserves people, expertise, biosphere, deep emplacement, the grid, and the food chain. It may destroy replaceable structures and equipment where that is cheaper than capture.

- Human opponents are intelligent and adaptive. Weather, terrain, canopy, underground spaces, submarines, commercial drones, fibre guidance, paper couriers, simple weapons, and institutional delay remain genuine limits.

# 1. Strategic Command and Industry

These assets are the campaign’s irreplaceable institutional and industrial core. They are deliberately kept away from close combat. Their failure would threaten the venture itself rather than a local operation.

## Wain — Interstellar carrier and charter headquarters

| **Location** | Distant cislunar / outer-Earth system; never a close-orbit battleship |
| **Manufacture** | Interstellar origin; not reproducible locally |

**Role.** The Wain carries the Seneschal, biological directors, the finite manifest, high-end fabrication tooling, and seed industry. It is a converted commercial carrier rather than a purpose-built warship.

**Employment.** It inserts behind the Moon, releases the industrial and logistics branches, and remains far enough from Earth that human direct-ascent weapons and most debris scenarios cannot threaten it. It does not provide routine orbital bombardment or surface fire support.

**Limitations.** It is a single strategic hull and cannot be replaced inside the charter. Its size, thermal signature, and industrial activity make permanent concealment impossible once the campaign begins.

**Fallback and dependencies.** Anchor depots and the Belt Reserve prevent a single cislunar accident from destroying every expedition asset, but neither can replace the Wain’s command population or complete manifest.

**Fallback and dependencies.** Human clean-room industry supplies packaging, boards, and lower-grade replacements where possible; irrecoverable components remain permanent losses.

# 2. Orbital, Entry, and Surface Access

Orbital systems provide command, timing, entry, and denial. They do not substitute for persistent surface presence. The revised design reduces the conspicuous pre-T-0 low-orbit population by grouping Quivers inside larger Coffer carriers until closer to use.

## Warden — Orbital command, relay, and reserve-strike platform

| **Status** | Confirmed canon |
| --- | --- |
| **Quantity** | 22 inherited |
| **Length / mass** | ~80 m / ~600 t |
| **Orbit** | 1,200–6,000 km, manoeuvring |
| **Role** | Laser relay, tracking, deorbit coordination, Coulter magazine |

**Role.** Wardens are the orbital backbone linking the Wain, Works, stratospheric systems, Monoliths, and surface networks through tight-beam optical communications.

**Employment.** They manage Quiver timing, orbital traffic, target updates, conflict avoidance, and the small Coulter reserve. Their higher orbits keep them beyond every fielded 2026 direct-ascent system.

**Limitations.** They are finite, visible after hostilities begin, and vulnerable to long-term debris or cislunar disruption. Their kinetic magazine is too small and blunt for routine warfare.

**Fallback and dependencies.** Wardens cross-link with one another; no single platform is a unique command node. Surface and stratospheric networks retain limited local autonomy during link interruption.

## Coffer — Multi-Quiver orbital staging carrier

| **Status** | New v2.1 platform |
| --- | --- |
| **Quantity** | Working estimate: 1,000–1,400 |
| **Payload** | 6–12 Quiver entry packages |
| **Deployment** | Higher or less conspicuous staging orbits |
| **Purpose** | Reduce the duration and count of suspicious low-orbit objects |

**Role.** Coffer addresses the largest unresolved pre-T-0 exposure: eleven thousand newly catalogued low-orbit objects appearing over several weeks.

**Employment.** Coffers carry multiple inert Quiver packages and release them into final entry orbits close to the Wave. Loads remain distributed across many carriers and planes so that a Coffer failure does not remove an entire region’s strike capacity.

*Working estimate. Average load of roughly nine Quivers would stage the inherited opening inventory without requiring 11,000 long-duration independent objects.*

**Limitations.** It is one-shot, has little ability to loiter, and becomes predictable once committed. The opening inventory cannot be recreated quickly enough for a second planetary Wave.

**Fallback and dependencies.** Harrows, Petrels, Strato-Hawks, and surface launchers deliver later effects. The reserve constellation is insurance, not a second opening campaign.

# 3. Stratospheric and Upper-Air Systems

The stratospheric tier provides persistent strategic functions, not universal atmospheric dominance. Weather remains the principal environmental limit, especially for downward directed energy and solar-electric persistence at high latitude.

## Aegis — Counter-ballistic directed-energy platform

| **Status** | Revised canon; renamed from Hayward |
| --- | --- |
| **Quantity** | 38 current v1.5 |
| **Station** | ~27–28.5 km |
| **Power** | Compact fusion; manifest-dependent |
| **Primary effect** | Boost-phase structural kill |
| **Thermal limit** | Finite cumulative beam time followed by recovery |

**Role.** Aegis protects the acquisition from strategic ballistic launch by engaging bright, thin-skinned missiles during boost.

**Employment.** Platforms are stationed over known launch regions before T-0 and remain essentially fixed because repositioning is slow. Their geometry is favourable once missiles climb above cloud.

**Limitations.** Cloud delays engagement, thermal recovery creates a second-wave vulnerability, and every beam director or fusion-core loss is permanent.

**Fallback and dependencies.** Picket interceptors cover leakage and thermal recovery; Coulters and physical site control remain the final backstops.

## Picket — Kinetic ballistic backstop

| **Status** | New v2.1 platform |
| --- | --- |
| **Quantity** | Working estimate: 2,000–5,000 interceptors; 100–250 racks |
| **Basing** | Separate stratospheric racks and later ground sites |
| **Guidance** | External cueing plus narrow terminal sensing |
| **Targets** | Boosting missiles, leakage, threats to Aegis |

**Role.** Picket prevents Aegis thermal recovery or retargeting limits from creating an uncontested second launch window.

**Employment.** Racks remain physically separate from Aegis platforms. Aegis and Lantern sensors provide early cueing; the interceptor performs a short high-acceleration terminal engagement against a highly visible target.

**Limitations.** Picket is less efficient than the beam against massed launches, depends on known strategic geometry, and introduces additional stratospheric logistics.

**Fallback and dependencies.** It complements rather than replaces Aegis. Ground control of launch facilities and human custody remain essential after the opening days.

# 4. Tactical Air and Swarm Warfare

This section is the main v2.1 correction. The tactical battlespace is a machine ecology rather than a set of isolated drones. Nests coordinate cheap local platforms; valuable sensor heads and decision systems are reused rather than embedded in every expendable airframe.

## Nest — Local swarm launch, repair, and coordination station

| **Status** | New v2.1 platform; central |
| --- | --- |
| **Quantity** | ~30,000 early; 100,000–150,000 peak working estimate |
| **Typical complement** | 50–300 aerial drones, 10–40 crawlers, interceptor racks |
| **Power** | Grid, local generation, batteries; no compact fusion required in most units |
| **Compute** | One capable local node coordinating many narrow systems |

**Role.** Nest turns hundreds of cheap drones into a persistent local system. It is more important than any individual microdrone.

**Employment.** Nests protect Kestrel sites, ports, substations, police stations, rail junctions, service boards, and mobile columns. They launch, recover, recharge, repair, update, and fuse local sensor data.

**Limitations.** A destroyed Nest degrades local coordination and creates a concentrated maintenance target. Later terrestrial Nests use lower-grade electronics and therefore support smaller swarms or more human supervision.

**Fallback and dependencies.** Mobile mini-Nests, neighbouring nodes, fibre links, and direct Herald control preserve partial operation after a local loss.

## Midge — Palm-sized reconnaissance, perching, and relay drone

| **Status** | New v2.1 platform; derived from New Dawn Needle |
| --- | --- |
| **Quantity** | Several million active; tens of millions produced |
| **Endurance** | 1–4 hours flying; days or weeks perched and sleeping |
| **Sensors** | Cheap optical/thermal packages; limited recognition |
| **Manufacture** | Locally mass-produced |

**Role.** Midge provides the close, disposable eyes that the original Pale force lacked.

**Employment.** It flies ahead of Stingers, perches on buildings and trees, maps routes, watches entrances, relays around structures, and performs local battle-damage assessment.

**Limitations.** Midges are noisy at close range, weather-sensitive, easily destroyed, and incapable of reliable independent lethal judgment. Their data volume can overwhelm local processing.

**Fallback and dependencies.** Nests fuse observations; Hounds investigate ambiguous high-value targets; Chapmans take over indoors and underground.

## Swift — Automated counter-drone interceptor

| **Status** | New v2.1 platform; revised New Dawn concept |
| --- | --- |
| **Quantity** | Hundreds of thousands active; millions of rounds/bodies |
| **Basing** | Nest and Rook racks |
| **Targets** | FPVs, loitering munitions, commercial drones, decoys |
| **Effect** | Small kinetic or directional fragmentation payload |

**Role.** Swift provides cheap local point defence against the principal surviving human tactical weapon: small autonomous and fibre-guided drones.

**Employment.** It remains in a launcher until passive sensors detect an approach. Short engagement time and local cueing reduce the need for expensive onboard autonomy.

**Limitations.** Decoy swarms can exhaust magazines, clutter complicates identification, and repeated launches reveal defended sites.

**Fallback and dependencies.** Wasp/Midge observation, physical screens, electronic denial, and Stinger patrols provide layered defence. Larger Rook interceptors handle aircraft-sized targets.

## Wasp — Unarmed contact, marking, and emergency drone

| **Status** | New v2.1 platform; restrained New Dawn restoration |
| --- | --- |
| **Quantity** | 500,000 to several million working range |
| **Payload** | Speaker/display, marker, small medical or message load |
| **Armament** | None lethal |
| **Manufacture** | Local |

**Role.** Wasp is the least threatening flying interface for crowds, checkpoints, emergencies, and routine public contact.

**Employment.** It gives directions, marks hazards, delivers documents or medication, identifies waiting areas, and provides an aerial perspective without deploying an armed platform.

**Limitations.** It is vulnerable, easy to spoof, and cannot resolve difficult political or legal interactions.

**Fallback and dependencies.** Stingers provide physical presence; Clerks and Reeves handle formal decisions; Darts remain stored until lethal force is separately authorized.

## Dart — Small lethal one-effect strike drone

| **Status** | New v2.1 platform; replaces extravagant Hornet concept |
| --- | --- |
| **Quantity** | Hundreds of thousands stored/available |
| **Payload** | One Bodkin-scale directional, penetrator, or kinetic effect |
| **Autonomy** | Target confirmation normally external |
| **Basing** | Nest racks, patrol vehicles, Petrels |

# 5. Strike Effects and Munitions

The effect library remains deliberately limited. The planning question is not “How large an explosion?” but “Which physical function must cease, and what is the cheapest reliable effect that produces that condition?”

# 9. Human Residual Drone and Weapon Systems

Human drone systems are a primary tactical threat after the Wave. By 2126, unmanned aircraft and ground machines are distributed through military units, agriculture, logistics, policing, industry, emergency services, and private ownership. The Company can destroy large bases and factories; it cannot eliminate every small platform, motor, battery, controller, printer, workshop, or local autonomy model.

| **Human system** |** Campaign role** |
| --- | --- |
| Reconnaissance quadcopters | Observe Company routes, patrols, gateways, and sensor placements. Extremely distributed and easily replaced. |
| Autonomous FPV strike drones | Attack Stingers, Hands, Forges, Heralds, Nests, vehicles, and human collaborators after launch without continuous control. |
| Fibre-guided FPVs | Operate inside Herald-jammed areas and dense urban terrain; limited by spool range, snagging, and visible fibre. |
| Perching ambush drones | Remain dormant in attics, vehicles, trees, drains, or rubble until a simple visual, acoustic, or physical trigger is satisfied. |
| Fixed-wing mapping drones | Cross Domesday gaps, survey routes, and carry small relay or courier payloads over long local distances. |
| Cargo drones | Move ammunition, food, batteries, medical supplies, and components without using sensor-covered roads. |
| Ground crawlers | Place or inspect charges, move through tunnels, attack vehicle undersides, and carry fibre or couriers. |
| Counter-drone interceptors | Attack Midges, Wasps, Chapmans, and exposed Hounds using local optical or autonomous guidance. |
| Decoy swarms | Exhaust Swifts and Rook magazines, reveal sensor locations, and create classification burden. |
| Residual MALE and runway drones | Large systems survive only briefly; detectable, runway-dependent, and easy for Shrike, Strato-Hawk, and Scythe to prosecute. |

**Human drone doctrine.** Defenders prepare streets, forests, tunnels, and landing approaches as machine ambush zones. Small drones observe, decoy, relay, and strike before human fighters expose themselves. The Company wins through better coordination, replacement, sensing, and infrastructure control, but the cheap exchange remains favourable enough to make drone defence a permanent occupation requirement.

**Conventional residual systems.** Manned aircraft, armour, artillery, submarines, mines, rifles, mechanical bombs, and paper couriers remain relevant where they exploit weather, terrain, concealment, or proximity. Large formations disappear by Month 19; small systems and individual violence remain for the entire charter.

# 12. Manifest Implications and Open Reconciliation Items

The v2.1 force is intentionally less dependent on irreplaceable hardware per tactical action, but it introduces new shipped requirements. The redesign is not complete until the manifest mass, dense-compute allocation, fusion-core count, and production schedule are recalculated together.

## Expected manifest shifts

- Reduce Shrike count from 96 to roughly 48–60, releasing compact fusion cores, beam directors, spares, and integration effort.

- Reduce full Monolith count to roughly 40–60 and transfer routine coverage to locally producible Beacons and Cairns.

- Replace the inherited 180,000-Harrow peak with a smaller active fleet and many simpler stored airframes.

- Allocate shipped mass to Works Delta metrology and clean repair modules, Gatehouse packages, reusable Hound sensor heads, and administrative compute.

- Do not solve the seeker problem simply by shipping more seekers. The doctrinal correction is reusable verification and dependency targeting.

- Increase standardized components across optical mounts, actuators, power electronics, thermal systems, compute enclosures, and communication buses.

- Reserve compact fusion for Aegis, selected Shrikes, strategic transport, and systems whose endurance genuinely changes the campaign. Most swarm nodes should use grid power, batteries, fuel, or larger locally manufacturable power systems.

## Items still requiring exact derivation

- Coffer mass, orbital transfer schedule, and acceptable consequence of multi-Quiver carrier loss.

- Exact Shrike reduction and whether saved beam directors become Aegis spares, Picket sensors, or reserve units.

- Monolith dimensions and lift architecture after the reduced fleet and later deployment doctrine.

- Dense-compute allocation among Nests, Clerks, Hound processing, Tithing regional nodes, and high-capability ground systems.

- Harrow, Verderer, Petrel, Razor, Herald, and Leech fleet counts after the machine-ecology redesign.

- Works Delta and Gatehouse manifest mass versus expected lives saved and charter schedule recovered.

- Human drone inventory assumptions for 2126 and the opening Wave target catalogue for military, civil, agricultural, and industrial unmanned systems.

- A full loss-and-replacement model through Month 71, including the Month 40 exhaustion of equivalent high-grade spares.
