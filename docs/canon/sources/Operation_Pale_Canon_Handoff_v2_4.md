## 1. Project identity

Operation: Pale is a hard-science-fiction alien planetary-acquisition scenario. Earth is a commercially valuable, already-emplaced industrial biosphere on an interstellar corridor. The Sol Development Company receives a six-year charter to deliver a productive, administered planet that no longer requires a major continuing military commitment.

The Company is coercive and colonial, but its behavior is institutional rather than theatrical. It values schedule, throughput, credibility, infrastructure continuity, and predictable counterparties. It does not need humanity to admire it; it needs Earth to keep functioning.

## 2. Core constraints

- **Technology:** roughly one century ahead of 2026 humanity. Interstellar travel is the sole major handwave.
- **No FTL communications:** the Legate cannot wait for home authorization on campaign timescales.
- **Charter:** six years.
- **Hard casualty ceiling:** total human deaths must remain below five percent of world population. Military casualties have no separate protected status.
- **Preservation doctrine:** preserve people and practical expertise, biosphere, deep emplacement, and continuity of grid/water/food/medicine/nuclear safety when these help the charter.
- **No magic cyber:** authentication is not defeated and cryptographic keys are not conjured from inaccessible systems.
- **Finite precision ecosystem:** compact high-density fusion, beam directors, high-grade seekers, and dense compute came on the finite manifest and cannot be reproduced freely by the Foundries.
- **Human competence:** humans disperse, deceive, exploit weather and terrain, retain submarines, use passive systems, improvise runways, build decoys, and preserve institutional legitimacy.

## 3. Current names and institutions

- **Caravan:** interstellar expedition carrier and charter headquarters.
- **Legate:** composite delegated executive authority.
- **Foundry Alpha:** volatiles, water, gases, propellant.
- **Foundry Beta:** metals, airframes, structures, munition bodies.
- **Foundry Gamma:** integration and higher-complexity assembly.
- **Foundry Delta:** precision maintenance, calibration, salvage of manifest-grade components.
- **Anchor depots:** distributed cislunar logistics nodes.
- **Belt Reserve:** distant strategic insurance, not routine campaign supply.

## 4. Strategic architecture

### 4.1 Orbital control

- **22 Wardens** form the high-orbit command, relay, navigation, traffic-control, and deorbit-coordination backbone.
- **Coffers** are skeletal Quiver staging carriers, not enclosed freighters.
- **Quivers** are disposable atmospheric-entry buses. Standard loads remain Scythes, Mattocks, sensors, decoys, or other specialized payloads.
- **Kestrels** are valuable reusable orbit-surface transports and never assault-land to create compliance.

### 4.2 Strategic air and missile layer

- **Aegis:** 38 counter-ballistic stratospheric platforms with finite thermal magazines.
- **Picket:** disposable strategic interceptors providing the second layer against ballistic leakage or Aegis saturation/recovery gaps.
- **Shrike:** clear-air aircraft engagement, sensor blinding, missile-seeker destruction, and point defence; not a through-weather runway killer.
- **Lantern:** unarmed persistent stratospheric ISR, relay, weather, and assessment network.

### 4.3 Opening counter-air layer — latest revision

The opening architecture no longer depends on hundreds of Storks already orbiting human airspace before H-hour.

- **Snares** remain runway/climb-out loitering interceptors.
- **Quiver-M** is the new specialized entry package that directly releases disposable **Merlins** into the lower atmosphere minutes before H-hour.
- Direct Merlins provide the dense low/medium-altitude interceptor screen during roughly the first one to two hours, when emergency scrambles and already-airborne aircraft matter most.
- Merlins near fuel depletion enter a designed final-expenditure mode against a valid aircraft or exposed military target, otherwise self-dispose in a sterile area.
- **Storks** become the reusable second echelon. They enter after H-hour in staggered waves carrying replacement Merlins only where resistance persists.
- This substantially delays the Stork recovery/refuelling deadline and reduces the number of reusable carriers exposed during the most dangerous opening phase.

**Working planning figures, not yet mass-reconciled:** approximately 2,100–2,700 direct-deployed Merlins from specialized Quivers; 480 total Storks remain the current working fleet, but only a minority need be airborne near H-hour.

### 4.4 Strike and pursuit layer

- **Scythe:** cheap fixed-target/component-kill glide munition, predominantly correlation-guided in the opening Wave.
- **Mattock:** heavy penetrator/demolition body for hardened structures and protected machinery.
- **Harrow:** reusable passive regional weapons carrier, typically at 12–16 km, externally cued and optimized for post-Wave strike and pursuit.
- **Vulture:** persistent anti-vehicle loitering munition for moving armor, convoys, launchers, artillery, emitters, and support vehicles.
- **Petrel:** later local tactical VTOL from secure Nests; not an opening strategic platform.
- **Strato-Hawk:** later elite second-stage aircraft from secure runways; absent from the first-hour architecture.

## 5. Revised H-hour doctrine

The old thirty-hour night-following Wave is superseded.

### H+0 to H+60 minutes — global strategic suppression

The first hour is a global *first cut*, not literally every strike worldwide arriving simultaneously. Orbital geometry still matters.

Objectives:

- prevent or contain strategic launches;
- collapse integrated air-defence networks;
- trap or destroy most combat aviation;
- immobilize major surface fleets and naval aviation;
- disable military satellite support and ground gateways;
- preserve enough national command and early warning to prevent humans from mistaking the event for a human nuclear attack;
- begin a global Notification at the same time as the operation.

### H+1 to H+6 — bulk military dismantling

The main target inventory is serviced: aircraft support, armor, artillery, radar, air defence, engineering, coastal missiles, drone-control and communications infrastructure, fleet combat systems, and other exposed military functions.

### H+6 onward — assessment, reattack, pursuit, negotiation

Lanterns, Domesday, Harrows, Vultures, and surviving local systems classify what remains and service urgent movers or emitters. Lower-priority survivors become part of the Hunt rather than prolonging the initial Wave.

## 6. Opening Notification

Current preferred message:

> This transmission is not of human origin. Military forces worldwide are being neutralized now. This is not an attack by any human state. Civilian population centres, hospitals, food systems, water systems, and electrical infrastructure are not targets. Do not launch strategic weapons. Leave disabled military facilities and equipment immediately. Further instructions will follow.

The purpose is nuclear deconfliction and public-order preservation, not giving military crews a protected evacuation period.

## 7. Human response: United States and allies

The United States does not respond as an isolated nation. Its worldwide basing, alliance relationships, intelligence networks, and standing multinational commands make the opening reaction international almost immediately.

### First hour

- NORAD integrates US and Canadian warning.
- NATO, Five Eyes, US Indo-Pacific relationships, and bilateral commands begin sharing observations.
- The US informs allies and adversaries that it has not launched strategic weapons and requests reciprocal declarations.
- Several coordinated allied probes against Harrows, Storks, Lanterns, or other atmospheric systems may achieve local tactical successes.
- These successes do not restore an integrated air picture because support systems, emitters, tanker/airborne-warning assets, command links, and runways are being removed simultaneously.

### H+1 to H+6

The US and major allies increasingly distinguish:

1. conventional national resistance — temporarily ineffective;
2. local tactical resistance — possible and occasionally successful;
3. long-term concealed resistance — potentially viable but strategically unresolved.

The United States begins using its alliance architecture as a surviving political and informational weapon even as that architecture ceases functioning as a coherent military machine.

### Day 1

The President orders an operational stand-down from exposed offensive movement while retaining nuclear custody, civil government, local safety functions, and hidden national options. A multinational emergency coordination framework begins to emerge around nuclear deconfliction, observation, infrastructure continuity, and negotiations.

### Day 2

A provisional allied coordination group becomes formal enough to issue common demands while preserving national sovereignty. Governments share threat information but do not necessarily reveal every surviving concealed military asset to one another. A negative-declaration system—confirming that strategic systems are stationary without revealing exact locations—becomes more plausible than a universal shared order of battle.

The Company tolerates and may prefer this political coordination because a small number of functioning counterparties is administratively cheaper than hundreds of isolated governments, while ensuring it does not regenerate into a functioning multinational military command.

## 8. Airfield access becomes the first major post-Wave political contest

The Company requires a distributed network of terrestrial aviation sites for:

- Stork fuel, inspection, Merlin reload, and later routine maintenance;
- Harrow recovery, wing inspection, battery conditioning, payload loading, and repair;
- later Strato-Hawk operations;
- local robotic logistics;
- eventually Kestrel gateways adjacent to—not dependent on—the runway.

It therefore issues a standardized **Airfield Qualification Protocol** rather than demanding sovereignty over a few giant bases.

### 8.1 Preferred political hierarchy

The Company prefers:

1. recognized national governments;
2. national-regional joint authorities;
3. regional or local authorities that demonstrably control a useful site when the national government cannot or will not cooperate.

It avoids automatically recognizing separatist claims because political fragmentation increases administrative cost.

Standard Company formula:

> Operational control of this site is recognized. No determination concerning territorial sovereignty or constitutional status is implied.

### 8.2 Cooperation creates leverage

Refusing an airfield does not exclude Company aviation. It transfers logistics, investment, political access, and regional influence to a neighboring jurisdiction.

This creates competition among governments and regions for:

- emergency power and fuel;
- communications restoration;
- protected cargo flights;
- medical transport;
- infrastructure repair;
- early reconstruction;
- direct Company contact.

### 8.3 Important scenario partners

**Cuba** is a strong early Western Hemisphere candidate because a government under severe resource pressure could plausibly see rapid cooperation as a way to obtain fuel, grid support, protected trade, and strategic relevance after the old regional military hierarchy collapses.

**Serbia** is the strongest current candidate for the first major continental-European gateway: politically transactional and militarily neutral in the scenario, centrally located in the Balkans, connected to European transport corridors, outside NATO command, and able to present cooperation as continuity of neutrality rather than ideological defection. The Company offers no territorial reward in Kosovo or elsewhere; early cooperation buys access and influence, not border revision.

Belarus is downgraded as an early candidate because of its close Russian military integration and the risk that Company basing there would be interpreted as intrusion directly into Russia's strategic security architecture.

## 9. Airfield activation doctrine

A cooperative airfield progresses through several layers rather than jumping directly to Kestrel operations.

### Stage A — nomination and site freeze

The offering authority provides runway maps, fuel status, security structure, hazards, and one responsible site commander. The site then enters a visibly non-hostile configuration: aircraft parked, weapons declared or removed, air-defence systems inactive, runway and firefighting services maintained, perimeter controlled.

### Stage B — expendable qualification package

Disposable Casks or equivalent low-value packages deliver the first autonomous layer:

- compact **Nest**;
- Midges and Stakes for observation;
- Crawlers and Ferrets for drains, culverts, tunnels, hangars, and confined spaces;
- Hands for human-designed machinery;
- a light Forge for clearing and repair;
- Rook/Swift/Stinger security;
- navigation beacons;
- fuel sampling and filtration equipment;
- optical communications.

### Stage C — robotic conversion

Hands operate human tools, fuel trucks, pumps, cranes, forklifts, hangar doors, and controls. Forges move wreckage, repair surfaces, emplace barriers, restore power, and prepare dedicated service zones. Human airport workers remain important because they know the local systems.

The efficient division is:

- **humans:** bulk labor, fuel production and transport, firefighting, utilities, local security, warehouses, civil engineering, and local knowledge;
- **Company robots:** verification, hazardous inspection, precision interfaces, autonomous aircraft handling, security against sabotage, and access to orbital supply.

### Stage D — first reusable aircraft

A Harrow or low-risk utility aircraft performs the first reusable landing. A Stork follows only after the site proves navigation, runway integrity, perimeter discipline, fuel quality, and command reliability.

### Stage E — routine Stork/Harrow operations

The field becomes a forward operating site with fuel, inspection, Merlin replacement, Harrow payload loading, and persistent robotic point defence.

### Stage F — Kestrel gateway

Only after repeated compliance does a Kestrel descend to a separate vertical landing pad carrying high-value diagnostic, precision, compute, spares, and manufacturing modules. Kestrels never create the site; they reward a site that already works.

## 10. Revised Stork fuel doctrine

The v2.3 Stork remains approximately 12–16 tonnes loaded, with roughly 30–42 hours maximum endurance under favorable profiles. That figure is an emergency ceiling, not a planned opening-sortie duration.

The direct-Merlin revision means:

- Storks no longer need to arrive 6–18 hours before H-hour;
- only a small contingency group may be near theatre at H-hour;
- first relief Storks enter roughly H+1 to H+4;
- later echelons enter after the Company knows where air resistance actually persists;
- first serious Stork recovery can move into late Day One rather than becoming a crisis around H+12.

Storks are deliberately compatible with terrestrial kerosene/heavy aviation fuel families after robotic testing, filtering, water separation, and connector adaptation. Importing thousands of tonnes of ordinary aircraft fuel from orbit would be strategically irrational.

## 11. Day-2 political endpoint

By H+48:

- the US President remains in office and retains civil legitimacy, much of the civilian state, and nuclear custody;
- conventional military freedom of action is gone;
- allies coordinate nuclear stand-down, infrastructure continuity, Company observation, and minimum negotiating conditions;
- governments still preserve separate concealed military options;
- Company restrictions on strategic movement have become a de facto global military air-traffic-control regime;
- the first competition for qualified Company logistics sites is beginning;
- human resistance has shifted from winning the opening battle to preserving legitimacy, options, knowledge, and bargaining leverage.

The key human strategic insight is:

> The Company can dismantle exposed military force much faster than it can replace functioning governments, alliance relationships, local authority, utility operators, and administrative legitimacy.

The key Company insight is symmetrical:

> Human political coordination is useful until it becomes military coordination again.

## 12. Firm later-campaign canon retained from earlier work

Unless deliberately revised later:

- precision-seeker scarcity remains a central campaign clock;
- human weather exploitation remains a genuine limitation;
- the Domesday remains a mapped sensor layer, not a planetary blanket;
- strategic submarines remain incompletely solved during the opening period;
- satellite/communications reconstruction takes months rather than weeks;
- commercial human operators retain credentials and keys;
- large-scale organized resistance ends much later than the opening victory;
- the six-year charter remains narrowly met in the older endpoint, pending a future full narrative rewrite.
