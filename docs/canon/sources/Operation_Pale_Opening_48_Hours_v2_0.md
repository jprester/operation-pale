# Operation: Pale — Opening 48 Hours v2.0

*Human-government narrative reconstruction after the compressed-Wave revision*

**Perspective:** primarily the President of the United States, senior US national-security leadership, and several key allies.  
**Canon status:** narrative synthesis consistent with Canon Handoff v2.4. Exact dialogue is illustrative rather than transcript canon.

## H−20 to H-hour — the impossible orbital picture

US and allied tracking networks see a rapidly worsening picture: mass orbital releases and deorbit commitments that do not fit a normal human strike pattern. The first hypotheses are still human—sensor failure, classified US activity, Russian or Chinese first strike preparation—because an extraterrestrial explanation is institutionally absurd until it stops being optional.

The President is moved into secure command facilities. NORAD, NATO, Five Eyes, US Indo-Pacific commands, and national strategic commands begin cross-checking observations. American forces overseas receive preliminary warnings at the same time as domestic commands.

The most important early presidential order is negative: **no strategic launch without verified evidence of a human nuclear attack.**

## H+0 — Notification

The Company message appears globally at the same time as the first operational actions:

> This transmission is not of human origin. Military forces worldwide are being neutralized now. This is not an attack by any human state. Civilian population centres, hospitals, food systems, water systems, and electrical infrastructure are not targets. Do not launch strategic weapons. Leave disabled military facilities and equipment immediately. Further instructions will follow.

Within minutes, observatories and military sensors verify non-human orbital behavior strongly enough that the White House cannot dismiss the message as deception.

The President's immediate priorities become:

- prevent nuclear misidentification;
- keep national command functioning;
- establish allied and adversary contact;
- preserve civil infrastructure;
- determine whether any coherent conventional military response still exists.

## H+0 to H+20 minutes — the opening cut

Aegis and Picket cover strategic launches. Quiver strikes begin dismantling airbases, air-defence nodes, strategic communications, naval aviation, exposed missile support, and military space gateways.

At the same time, specialized Quiver-M packages release disposable Merlins directly into lower-atmosphere patrol boxes. These cover the emergency-scramble interval without requiring Storks to have spent hours on station before H-hour.

The counter-air layers now overlap:

- **Snare:** runway, alternate-strip, and climb-out geometry;
- **direct Merlin:** low/medium-altitude scramble interception and bad-weather coverage;
- **Shrike:** clear/high air, sensor blinding, missile-seeker destruction;
- **Scythe/Mattock:** parked aircraft, hardened shelters, radars, fuel, maintenance, and command support.

The effect is not that no human aircraft gets airborne. Some do. The effect is that successful local sorties fail to regenerate a theatre-wide air picture.

## H+10 to H+30 — allies become operationally inseparable

The US response is international from the beginning.

### Canada / NORAD

Canada and the United States treat the continental warning problem as one problem. The key question is whether any human ballistic force is actually launching.

### NATO

NATO military staffs begin exchanging reports before political leaders can formally convene. US bases in Europe are struck together with host-nation air and command systems. The alliance's interoperability briefly improves human tactical reaction—then becomes a vulnerability as combined air-operations centers, long-range surveillance, tanker fleets, airborne warning, and communications nodes are targeted.

### Five Eyes

The US, UK, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand pool radar, optical, signals, and astronomical evidence. This network becomes one of the first human mechanisms capable of independently verifying that the attacker is global and external.

### Japan / South Korea / western Pacific

The daylight timing creates heavy initial activity. American and allied bases are attacked together. Regional commands face the additional danger that North Korea, China, Japan, South Korea, or the US could misread one another's strategic movement during the crisis.

## H+20 to H+90 — the first coalition probes

Human commanders attempt several coordinated attacks rather than surrender immediately.

Some work tactically. A passive cue may allow a battery to destroy a Harrow. A multinational air-defence engagement may damage a Stork or force a Shrike to alter station geometry. Fighters may survive by staying low in cloud or using improvised recovery fields.

The Company's advantage is not magical invulnerability. It is that local human successes do not accumulate into restored operational coherence. The shooter emits, moves, requires fuel, or returns to an observed support system. Harrows and Vultures exploit the resulting pattern.

The Pentagon gradually separates three questions:

1. Can the US restore conventional national military freedom of action? **Not in the opening conditions.**
2. Can humans still kill Company systems locally? **Yes.**
3. Can hidden, submarine, passive, special-operations, and distributed capabilities survive for later use? **Probably.**

## H+1 to H+3 — the President shifts objectives

The President receives reports from North America, Europe, the Pacific, the Gulf, and fleets at sea. The pattern is consistent: exposed combat systems are being dismantled globally while cities, grids, hospitals, and civilian administration remain broadly intact.

A key realization enters the room:

> They are not attacking the country. They are attacking the state's ability to use organized force.

The US sends a challenge through the authenticated contact path. The Company answers while simultaneously performing an independently observable Warden maneuver, binding the digital channel to physical control.

The United States states that it has ordered no strategic launch and asks what conditions halt further attacks.

The Company demands immediate strategic non-movement, cessation of offensive sorties, preservation of utilities and nuclear custody, and maintenance of public order.

The President does not surrender. He orders an **operational stand-down from exposed offensive movement** while retaining civil institutions, nuclear custody, local safety forces, and undisclosed national options.

## H+3 to H+6 — first public address and the second shock

The President publicly confirms that the attack is not known to originate from any human state. He tells civilians to stay away from military sites and emphasizes that utilities and hospitals are functioning.

Several isolated US and allied units continue fighting, partly because they distrust communications and partly because some commanders believe any stand-down is premature.

A few score real successes. They matter psychologically. They do not change the strategic balance.

The central military problem is no longer simply destruction. It is **loss of support architecture**. A surviving fighter without tanker support, integrated warning, secure mission data, maintenance, and a safe recovery strip is an inventory item rather than usable airpower.

## H+6 to H+12 — daylight and the coalition picture

Images spread globally: aircraft intact but mechanically destroyed, radar faces perforated, hangars opened, localized fires at military fuel and ammunition sites, civilian neighborhoods beside bases mostly intact.

Public fear shifts from nuclear annihilation to uncertainty about political control.

US and allied governments establish an emergency coordination framework centered on:

- mutual confirmation of no strategic launch;
- shared Company observations;
- military stand-down procedures;
- infrastructure continuity;
- common negotiating principles;
- prevention of opportunistic interstate action.

Russia and China are not allies, but limited strategic deconfliction becomes unavoidable. India and Pakistan become a particular concern because neither wants the Company crisis to become cover for human strategic advantage.

## H+12 — interim Company terms

The Company sends governments operational conditions rather than demanding immediate abolition of sovereignty.

Typical terms include:

- no strategic launch or dispersal;
- no unauthorized military aircraft takeoff;
- no major armed naval sortie;
- restrictions on long-range targeting radar;
- continuous staffing of grid, water, hospitals, food distribution, and reactors;
- maintenance of public order;
- nomination of remote contact and logistics sites;
- continued surveillance and action against strategic movement.

The President sends a conditional acceptance of the operational restrictions while explicitly refusing to recognize Company sovereignty.

The Company records the claim as unresolved and moves on.

## H+12 to H+18 — military preservation and the first logistics discussions

The Joint Chiefs issue a preservation order:

- stop exposed offensive movement;
- maintain nuclear custody;
- separate personnel from targetable equipment where practical;
- preserve medical, engineering, firefighting, and transport functions;
- retain records and intelligence;
- do not independently negotiate political terms.

Meanwhile the Company begins asking for **airfield nominations**.

This is the first point where the post-Wave political economy becomes visible. A government that offers a secure runway may receive early cargo access, fuel assistance, communications, and reconstruction. A government that refuses may simply watch a neighboring jurisdiction become the regional gateway.

## H+18 to H+24 — coalition politics

The United States uses its alliance network as the surviving skeleton of a human response.

An improvised allied coordination group demands:

- symmetrical restrictions on all human states;
- preservation of functioning civil governments;
- protected humanitarian movement;
- strategic verification;
- hazard maps and debris data;
- no automatic political rewards for collaboration;
- no forced transfer of cryptographic keys.

The Company accepts many of these because they lower administrative cost.

At H+24, the President still controls the civil state, much of federal administration, emergency services, and the nuclear custody chain—but not unrestricted military airspace, fleet movement, strategic-force dispersal, or long-range military sensing.

The United States has not formally surrendered. It has entered an operational armistice under observation.

## Day Two, H+24 to H+30 — from battle to order

The President's morning briefing is clearer and more disturbing than Day One's.

Most exposed combat aviation is destroyed, grounded, or operationally orphaned. Major naval vessels survive physically but many are immobilized or blind. Air defence exists in isolated pockets rather than a national network. Strategic weapons remain dangerous but increasingly stationary.

Canada largely tracks US restrictions but insists on separate recognition of Canadian authority. Britain focuses on concealed strategic submarines. Japan demands symmetry against neighboring states before accepting long-term aviation restrictions. Australia offers relatively intact communications and southern-hemisphere observation infrastructure.

The allied coordination group becomes formal enough to negotiate but deliberately avoids becoming a single supranational military command.

## H+30 — the Company formalizes military movement categories

The Company announces that integrated global military opposition is no longer coherent and establishes:

- **compliant systems:** stationary, powered down, separated from immediate combat use;
- **safety-authorized movement:** firefighting, nuclear safety, EOD, medical evacuation, disaster relief, civilian-aviation recovery;
- **hostile movement:** strategic dispersal, unauthorized combat aviation, active long-range targeting radar, armed naval sortie, anti-orbital preparation, formation-level military concentration.

The world now has a de facto Company military traffic-control regime.

## H+30 to H+36 — overseas bases and airfields

US overseas installations expose the legal complexity of the new order. A base in Germany, Japan, Britain, Italy, or elsewhere may involve a US commander, a host government, an alliance command, and Company restrictions simultaneously.

The Company refuses to adjudicate constitutional theory. It demands one consistent operating plan and one authority capable of enforcing it at the site.

In parallel it publishes or privately distributes an **Airfield Qualification Protocol**. Governments and regional authorities can nominate runways for Harrow recovery, Stork servicing, humanitarian aviation, and later Kestrel logistics.

## H+36 to H+42 — the political competition begins

Some governments see Company airfield access as occupation. Others see it as a route to fuel, power, trade, and strategic relevance.

Cuba is a plausible early Caribbean gateway in the scenario. The political bargain is not ideological conversion: offer a runway and secure perimeter; receive emergency energy, communications, protected cargo, and early reconstruction.

Serbia is a particularly strong candidate for the first major continental-European gateway. Its scenario value comes from central geography, infrastructure, transactional balancing, and its ability to present cooperation as continuity of military neutrality. The Company explicitly refuses to trade territorial revision for access.

The political lesson reaches Washington quickly: refusing a Company site in the US does not keep Company aircraft away. It transfers the base, benefits, and influence to another jurisdiction.

## H+42 to H+48 — the second-day equilibrium

Congress, allied legislatures, and national courts begin asking unprecedented questions about the legal status of compliance under overwhelming force.

The President seeks emergency authority to coordinate infrastructure, military stand-down, negotiations, and site access without calling the arrangement surrender.

At the same time, intelligence and military leaders begin separating overt continuity from concealed national options. Allies preserve government records, scientific observation, communications, and leadership succession openly while retaining submarines, special operations, hardened nodes, and other residual military capabilities within national compartments.

By H+48, the strategic situation is:

- the Company has won the opening conventional contest;
- human governments survive because the Company needs their administrative capacity;
- the US remains the most important coordinator of its alliance system, but that system is now political and informational more than military;
- nuclear deconfliction is a shared human priority;
- airfield qualification is becoming the first material reward for cooperation;
- the next contest is over who provides the first durable surface gateways and under what political terms.

The President's core realization at the end of Day Two is simple:

> The Company can destroy exposed force quickly. It cannot run eight billion people, hundreds of governments, utility grids, courts, ports, hospitals, and supply chains by itself.

That dependency is not enough to reverse the military defeat. It is enough to create bargaining space.
