# Operation: Pale — The Invasion Narrative v1.5

*The Pale: the bounded territory within which a holder's writ runs, and outside which it does not, yet.*
*An occupation, executed against a deadline, by a party that needs the planet working when it finishes — and that runs out of things.*

---

## A Note on the Names

The operation's internal designators have no English equivalents. The translation model that produced the human-readable set drew on the vocabulary of medieval English land administration, because it was the only body of language that distinguished cleanly between the offices required: one who surveys, one who mends, one who keeps stock inside the bounds, one who reaps. The names stuck. Humans found them archaic and quaint for about three days.

## Ground Rules

1. **One century of advantage, and the constraint is enforced downward.** The acquirer fields capability roughly equivalent to 2126 humanity. Nothing at Earth violates known physics. The interstellar drive is the sole handwave, and — critically — it does not imply everything else scales with it. A civilisation that can cross the gap still has to build seekers one at a time.

2. **No faster-than-light communication.** A courier home runs two to three months each way. The Seneschal governs with wide discretion and cannot be corrected in time to matter. **Nothing shipped can be replaced inside the campaign.**

3. **The schedule is the binding constraint, and it is external.** Six years from arrival to a planet that is producing, administered, and requires no further military commitment. Missing the date does not lose the planet; it loses the venture its economics and the Seneschal its charter.

4. **Preserve only what cannot be re-created inside the schedule.** Four categories: people and what they know; the biosphere; deep emplacement (mines, dams, dredged channels, graded rail beds, cleared farmland — volume-of-earth problems that resist a century of advantage); and continuity of life support, meaning the grid and food chain stay up because people die during the outage. Everything else is expendable.

5. **The casualty ceiling is five per cent.** Four hundred million.

6. **Two industries, and only one of them is here.** This governs more of the campaign than any weapon.

   The **Works** at Earth-Moon L2 can make anything whose difficulty is *mass*: airframes, hulls, structure, propellant, warhead bodies, bulk alloy, wiring, actuators, large fusion plants, and simple correlation optics. Its output ramps from 90 tonnes a day at month six to 1,400 tonnes a day by T-0, and it grows from a captured carbonaceous body.

   It cannot make anything whose difficulty is *miniaturisation and precision*. Compact high-power-density fusion cores, adaptive-optics beam directors, high-grade imaging seekers, and dense compute all require an industrial ecosystem — elemental separation to parts-per-billion, semiconductor fabrication, optical figuring to fractions of a wavelength, and the quality control apparatus around all of it — that does not bootstrap from an asteroid in nineteen months and that the acquirer did not attempt to build here.

   Those come from home, on **the manifest**: three thousand seven hundred and sixty tonnes, shipped once, and there is no second delivery inside six years.

   **Every hard decision in this operation is a decision about the manifest.**

7. **Humans fight well and are the best-armed subject population ever acquired.** Courage operates inside an envelope. The operation does not attack courage, and it does not always beat it.

8. **Nothing is emplaced on Earth's surface before T-0 except sensors**, for reasons given in earlier annexes: half a million adhered objects on daily-inspected equipment produces a discovery, and one photograph converts a surprise against a peacetime planet into an assault on an alerted one.

9. **Nuclear release against the acquirer is unlikely but not negligible.** A warhead needs a target and an orbital opponent presents none. The genuine risk is humanity firing at itself once the international order and every deterrence assumption underneath it have gone.

10. **Nothing is decrypted and no authentication is defeated.** Not from restraint — it is never the shortest path. Every system the acquirer wants is operated by people in buildings inside territory it administers.

| Spares and reserve | 926 t | Consumed by month 40. |

The distinction that runs through the entire campaign is between the two seeker grades.

A **correlation seeker** is made at the Works by the million. It compares what it sees to a stored image of a specific place and flies at it. Against a tank sitting in a surveyed vehicle park at known coordinates, it is entirely sufficient, because the target has agreed to be where it was yesterday.

A **precision seeker** recognises a thing rather than a place: it can find a tarped vehicle under canopy in terrain that has changed since the survey. There are three hundred and forty thousand of them and there will never be any more.

The Wave spends none. The hunt spends all of them.

## Phase 0: Emplacement (T-19 months to T-0)

### The Works

The **Wain** inserts behind the Moon and is not detected. Over nineteen months the Works grow around a nudged carbonaceous body, and the ramp is slow: 90 tonnes a day at month six, 620 at month twelve, 1,400 by T-0. Refining, alloying, and structural fabrication scale readily; nothing else does, and the Seneschal's requests home for a second manifest are dispatched twice and answered neither time.

Detection is partial and priced. Six anomalous infrared sources at lunar distance enter survey data between T-14 and T-3 months. A working group reviews them twice and returns *uncharacterised, probably instrumental*. An astronomer in Atacama is certain they are artificial and cannot get anyone with authority to care. She is right about everything and it changes nothing.

### The Domesday, and its Limits

The **Domesday** is a dispersed sensor substrate, and v1.4 of this plan described a blanket. It is not a blanket. Blanketing a planet at metre resolution requires an element mass and a power, position, sensing, and meshing solution that no gram-scale device has, and the Works could not have made enough of them in any case.

What is actually seeded is four hundred billion elements at eight-tenths of a gram — three hundred and twenty tonnes — distributed by high-altitude release and prevailing wind **against a map**, not across the surface:

- **High density** along trunk road networks, around military installations, at bridges and chokepoints, and across pre-identified dispersal terrain. Roughly two per cent of land area.
- **Useful density** across a further nine per cent: secondary roads, industrial areas, coastlines, valley systems.
- **Nothing at all** across the remaining eighty-nine per cent of the land surface, and nothing over most of the ocean.

The map is the doctrine. Military vehicles are large, heavy, and roadbound; they park in known places and move along a small number of routes. The acquirer does not need to see everywhere. It needs to see where things must pass.

*Honest limits, unchanged and now load-bearing:* the Domesday does not go indoors, underground, inside filtered enclosures, or under dense canopy. It thins in heavy rain and is destroyed by fire. A unit that leaves the mapped corridors is a unit the Domesday has lost — and the map's edges are, by month eight, published in the resistance press.

### The Tithing

The gap is filled by something the acquirer does not have to build.

Earth carries roughly one billion one hundred million fixed cameras, forty million number-plate readers and traffic sensors, six hundred million connected vehicles reporting position and telemetry, and building management systems in most structures above four storeys — all of it running on networks the acquirer takes on the first day along with the satellite ground segment and the terrestrial telecoms.

**The Tithing** is that apparatus, fused. It requires no new hardware, no emplacement, and no risk. It costs only compute, which is on the manifest, and which is why the fleet ceiling is nine hundred thousand platforms rather than three million.

Where the Domesday is best — open country, roads, dispersal terrain — the Tithing is worthless. Where the Domesday fails — cities, buildings, interiors, the dense human world — the Tithing is extraordinary. The two are exact complements and neither was designed as one.

The name is the translation model's, and it is precise. In Anglo-Saxon law a tithing was a group of ten households bound to observe one another and answer for what they saw. The acquirer occupies the planet substantially through the surveillance apparatus the planet built to watch itself, and does not have to modify a single camera.

### The Constellation and the Lampreys

Eleven thousand **Sheafs** are lifted into sun-synchronous planes phased so servicing passes fall between 0100 and 0500 local. This is the cheapest lever on the casualty ceiling and it costs only a choice of orbit. The constellation is visible and no attempt is made to hide it; by T-4 days the working assumption in two capitals is that a third has done something and is not admitting it. This remains the largest pre-T-0 exposure and it is unsolved.

**Lampreys** attach to submarine hulls. Fourteen SSBNs are at sea at T-0. An earlier annex claimed eleven carried Lampreys, which was the most optimistic figure in the plan and was never justified: a ballistic-missile submarine exists precisely to defeat this, changes depth, speed, and pattern, monitors acoustic contacts continuously, and operates inside protected water alongside friendly anti-submarine forces.

**Four are tagged.** Ten are not, and the submarine problem is therefore not solved at T-0 and will not be solved for most of a year.

## Phase 1: The Wave (T+0:00 to T+30:00)

### T+0:00 — The Notification

There is no deception and no silence, and the reason is not principle. A thirty-hour rolling campaign generates enormous ambiguity, and ambiguity is the only weapon on Earth capable of hurting the acquirer — not because a warhead can reach it, but because a blinded, confused nuclear power may fire at a *neighbour*.

*"This transmission is not of human origin.*

*"Beginning now and continuing for approximately thirty hours, we will destroy military equipment across your planet. We will work region by region as we pass overhead. We have arranged the sequence so that most of it happens between one and five in the morning, local time, where you are.*

*"Get out of your vehicles, your aircraft, and your ships. Move two hundred metres away and stay there. We are going to destroy them and you do not need to be inside them when we do. This is the only warning we will give and it is sufficient.*

*"Your cities are not targets. Your power, water, and food systems will not be interfered with. Nothing is descending toward any populated area. Your early-warning systems are functioning and will continue to function; look at them. Nothing is inbound. No nation on your planet has attacked another.*

*"We will destroy satellite ground stations. We will not harm any satellite. We will rebuild the ground stations. Evacuate them.*

*"Do not launch. There is nothing to launch at.*

*"We will now begin. The first regions are as follows."*

And it names them, in order, with times.

The list is accurate, and this matters more than any weapon the acquirer owns. By hour twelve, national broadcasters in countries not yet reached are reading the schedule aloud as a public safety announcement.

Simultaneously, over every capital at fifteen kilometres, a **Bound** becomes visible — large, motionless, lit. It does nothing for six years except be seen.

### The Wave

Sheafs enter at Mach 22, decelerate behind ablative fairings, and release at twenty-two kilometres. Around 1.2 million **Scythes** glide to individually assigned aimpoints, every one of them carrying a locally-made correlation seeker flying at a surveyed coordinate. Twelve thousand **Mattocks** open what the Scythes cannot: hardened shelters, blast doors, tunnel portals, and the flight decks of nuclear-powered carriers that have no propulsion intakes for a Scythe to enter.

The aimpoint doctrine is unchanged and the crew-survival argument is its entire justification: engine decks, breech rings, wing roots, antenna arrays, intake plenums. Salvage is a consequence, not a reason.

### T+3:40 — Grafenwöhr

The German battalion commander has done exactly what the broadcast told him to do, which he will spend the rest of his life explaining and never quite justifying to himself. His vehicles are dispersed across the hardstanding, shut down and empty. His crews are standing in the treeline three hundred metres away in the cold, watching.

At 0347 there is no sound at all until the last two seconds. Fifty-one impacts arrive within a nine-second window, each a flat hard crack from an engine deck. No fireball, no secondary detonation, no scream. When the smoke clears there are fifty-one Leopard 2s and Pumas standing in perfect order, guns level, optics unmarked, radios still muttering on internal power, and not one of them will ever move again.

A gunner beside him says, *they didn't even damage them.* The commander understands that this is the worst thing about it and cannot explain why.

Eleven kilometres away, at 0351, the hardened shelters at the airfield are opened by Mattocks. That sounds like what it is.

### The Warned Third, and the Cost

By hour twelve the far side of the planet has had half a day to think. Most units comply, and compliance above eighty per cent in the later regions is the largest single reason the toll stays where it does. Roughly a hundred and forty thousand platforms survive by dispersing, hiding, and breaking the survey. Some fight, and the ones that fight die at rates the compliant do not.

**Wave casualties, thirty hours: approximately 340,000.**

An earlier annex put this at 206,000, which was a rate of four-tenths of a death per platform serviced. That figure is defensible for a warned night strike against empty vehicles and is not defensible across the daylight third, the sortieing air forces, the ships at sea, the three hundred and forty ground-segment compounds, and the ordinary friction of destroying half a million machines in a day and a quarter. The revised figure is 0.68 per platform and is probably still low.

## Phase 2: The Air War the Acquirer Did Not Expect to Have

The **Fowler** was designed to sit at twenty-six kilometres above an airfield and kill aircraft in the take-off roll with a directed-energy mount. The design justification was the Hayward's: at that altitude ninety-eight per cent of the atmosphere is *below* the platform, so beam attenuation and thermal blooming are negligible.

**That justification is correct for the Hayward and false for the Fowler, and the error was not caught until month two.**

A rising ballistic missile climbs *into* the thin air, and the engagement happens along a short path most of which is above the weather. An aircraft on a runway is at the bottom of the entire lower atmosphere, and a beam fired downward at it traverses every metre of the turbulence, moisture, aerosol, and thermal structure that makes ground-attack directed energy hard in the first place. The air above the platform is irrelevant. It was reasoning by analogy, in a plan whose whole method was supposed to be the opposite, and it survived three revisions of the annex.

**What the Fowler can actually do**, and this is not nothing:

- Engage aircraft that have climbed above the weather into clear air — which is where they must go to be militarily useful, and where the path is short and clean.
- **Blind.** Destroying an optical or infrared sensor, or a missile seeker, requires orders of magnitude less energy on target than burning through structure, and works through atmosphere that would defeat a structural kill entirely. A Fowler cannot reliably kill an aircraft climbing through cloud. It can reliably ruin every seeker and sensor that aircraft is carrying, from beyond any range at which the aircraft can respond.

**What kills aircraft on runways is the Springe**, promoted from weather fallback to primary: a 34-kilogram ducted-electric interceptor loitering at eight hundred to three thousand metres, beneath the weather, engaging aircraft on the roll and in low climb-out with kinetic darts. It is slow, short-legged, and has a few seconds of engagement window. It costs six minutes of Works output, it carries a crude seeker, and there are fourteen thousand of them because they can be made here.

Ninety-six Fowlers, not two hundred and ninety. The manifest would not carry more.

**And the humans work it out.** By month four, three air forces have independently established that sorties launched into weather survive, and that the thing at twenty-six kilometres cannot do to them what it does in clear air. Roughly twenty-nine hundred combat sorties are flown against the occupation over six years, mostly in bad weather, mostly at night, mostly achieving very little — and mostly returning. They kill four Fowlers, one Hayward, two Carters, eleven thousand Beadles, and a hundred and forty Springes.

It is the only domain in which humans discover a real limitation and exploit it systematically, and the acquirer's honest assessment is that it never closed the hole. It waited the aircraft out. Airframes need spares, fuel, and runways, and all three run down over four years while the acquirer watches, and the last recorded combat sortie against the occupation is flown in month fifty-one from a stretch of motorway in Anatolia by an aircraft that had been rebuilt twice from three others.

## Phase 3: The Nuclear Question (Day 1 to Month 9)

**Thirty-eight Haywards, not fifty.** The manifest set the number and the number is short of what the earlier plan called for, which means some silo fields have two platforms overhead rather than twelve.

**And the Hayward has a magazine, which the earlier annexes did not admit.** A 3.1-megawatt optical beam implies roughly six megawatts of waste heat, and a stratospheric aircraft rejects heat into air at two per cent of sea-level density. Cumulative beam-on time is limited to about forty seconds, after which the platform must stop for ninety minutes.

Within a single boost window this does not bind — the retarget cycle limits it to twelve to twenty kills first. **Across successive windows it binds absolutely.** A silo field that salvoed in two waves ninety minutes apart would have found the second wave unopposed.

Nobody does this. Command fragmentation, degraded communications, and the fact that authorities capable of coordinating a two-wave salvo were exactly the authorities that stood down mean the counter is never attempted. The Seneschal's risk register carries it at the top of the unexploited column for all six years, described in one line: *available to them at no cost, requiring only that they wait.*

**Four launches occur.** Three are killed in boost and their reentry vehicles come down across the territory of the states that launched them, producing four contamination footprints and approximately a hundred and eighty thousand deaths over six years, mostly from displacement and the collapse of medical provision inside the exclusion zones. The acquirer publishes every map the same evening, in full.

The fourth is launched from a field where the two Haywards overhead are in thermal recovery from the third. It completes its trajectory and detonates as programmed above an ocean target box, four hundred kilometres from anything, against a fleet that had been alongside and immobilised for eleven days. Nobody is killed. The acquirer says so publicly within the hour, and the fact that a warhead reached its aimpoint and achieved nothing is a more effective argument against further launches than any interception was.

### The Boats

Ten submarines are untagged, and the hunt takes seven months.

The **Sounder** grid finds six over the first fourteen weeks and **Otters** shadow them until their captains, out of food and out of a world to return to, surface. Three more are located between months four and seven.

The tenth is never found. It surfaces in month nine, five hundred kilometres south-west of the Azores, because its crew had been at sea for two hundred and eighty-one days and had eaten everything aboard. Its commander had read his Letter of Last Resort in the first week, had verified that his release codes were valid, had confirmed that his government was intact and had issued no order, and had concluded that the Letter's condition was not met.

He is right, and it is a near thing, and the operation never learns how near. Nobody on the acquirer's side records his name. The Seneschal's model had assigned an eleven per cent probability to a full salvo from an untagged boat, and eleven per cent of a Vanguard's load is a number that would have ended the venture, the ceiling, and the planet's near-term habitability. The reason it did not happen is that one man in the Atlantic made a judgement, over two hundred and eighty-one days, while nobody was able to find him.

### Month 3 — The Thing It Cannot Reach

The acquirer holds the orbit, the spectrum, the air, and the ground. It has no leverage at all over two governments in South Asia who, three months into a world with no international order, no mediating powers, and — because of the acquirer's own destruction of the ground segment — badly degraded strategic communications, arrive at a border crisis neither would have risked before.

One low-yield device is used. Approximately two hundred thousand people die.

The acquirer's response is the only genuinely improvised thing it does in six years: it broadcasts, on every band in both countries, a complete and accurate account of both sides' remaining deployments, on the theory that two governments that can each see the other is not preparing a follow-on strike may stop. It works, or something does. Nobody establishes which.

The estimate's line reads *residual sovereign risk, unmitigable*, and it is not entirely honest, because the interdiction that denied an insurgent communications network is also what left two nuclear neighbours reading each other's intentions through rumour.

## Phase 4: The Hunt, and the End of the Magazine (Day 2 to Month 19)

A hundred and forty thousand platforms survived the Wave by dispersing. The campaign against them is the longest military phase of the operation and it is the one the acquirer nearly loses to arithmetic.

### The Trap With Two Jaws

Concentrate and use the equipment, and you present a coherent target to an opponent with continuous overhead coverage. Stay dispersed, sheltered, and silent, and you have preserved your equipment by making it useless. There is no third posture — but the second one is *much* better than the earlier plan allowed, because the Domesday is a map and the map has edges, and because finding a tarped vehicle under canopy costs a precision seeker.

### The Magazine

Every hunting engagement against a hidden target consumes one to three of three hundred and forty thousand irreplaceable seekers.

Consumption runs far ahead of forecast from month three, for reasons that are all foreseeable in hindsight: dispersal is more widespread than modelled, the Domesday's mapped edges are identified and exploited faster than expected, and the miss rate against decoys is thirty-one per cent rather than the eleven assumed. Decoys in particular are a genuine human success — inflatable and heated dummies, previously a marginal Cold War technique, become the single most cost-effective thing anyone does against the occupation, at a materials cost of about forty euros per seeker destroyed.

**The magazine is exhausted in month fourteen.**

After month fourteen the acquirer cannot find things that hide. It can still find things that move, emit, mass, or use roads — the Domesday, the Tithing, and the Crier cover all of that — but a tarped vehicle in a forest is, from month fourteen, invisible to it for the remainder of the campaign.

What replaces the magazine is slower, cheaper, and much more like ordinary war:

- **Lymers** — ground search platforms, 40 kilograms, quadrupedal, working in packs of six under Verderer overwatch, carrying crude optics and a chemical sensor tuned to diesel, hydraulic fluid, and human occupancy. A Lymer finds nothing at range. It finds things by walking past them, at four kilometres an hour, across terrain, for as long as it takes, and there are two hundred thousand of them because they cost nine minutes each and use no shipped components at all.
- **The Tithing**, applied to the human world the hidden formations depend on. A dispersed battalion still needs food, fuel, and medical care, and those come from villages with cameras in them.
- **Time.** The acquirer stops hunting the equipment and starts waiting for it to need something.

Organised armed resistance above battalion scale ends in **month nineteen** — eight months later than the earlier plan projected, and the delay is the direct cost of a finite manifest and an honest sensor.

### Karabük — Day 61

A mechanised division disperses into broken country, runs on sealed depot fuel, maintains total electronic silence, tarps and shelters its vehicles, and moves only at night under canopy. Its commander has correctly identified every one of the acquirer's advantages and countered the ones he can.

It buys him fifty-one days.

The Domesday's custody chain holds on thirty-eight per cent of his vehicles — the earlier plan claimed sixty — and thirty-eight is not enough, so the division is found the expensive way: eleven thousand precision seekers, four hundred Lymer-days, and a Tithing correlation on a bakery in a town of nine hundred that was suddenly buying eleven times its normal flour.

The division is killed over nine days. Six thousand four hundred dead. There is no surrender because after the ninth day there is no formation left to surrender.

The Seneschal enters the seeker expenditure and does not total the other line.

### Cities

Urban resistance is the one problem altitude cannot solve. The operation does not besiege, bomb, cut power, or starve; it goes in on the ground with numbers and accepts losses.

**Beadles** — six hundred thousand, not two million, because the manifest caps compute — occupy streets, block movement, disarm individuals, and above all get shot at instead of anything expensive. **Verderers** hold armed overwatch at two thousand metres with acoustic arrays that localise a rifle shot to four metres in half a second, and they are the acquirer's largest recurring loss: captured man-portable air defence takes eleven thousand of them, more than every other armed platform combined.

**Warreners** work the tunnels, basements, metros, and entombed complexes, trailing forty kilometres of fibre because neither the Domesday nor radio goes underground, and doing more of the counter-insurgency work of years three through six than anything with a gun.

## Phase 5: The Occupation (Month 2 to Year 6)

### The Orbital Estate

The acquirer needs the constellations working; its own ceiling runs through shipping, finance, and pharmaceutical logistics, all of which run through orbit. The leverage is orbital mechanics: a geostationary satellite drifts out of every fixed user antenna's pointing envelope within eight to sixteen weeks of losing ground control, a five-hundred-and-fifty-kilometre constellation loses phasing in months and deorbits inside five years, and a twelve-hundred-kilometre satellite dies of accumulated unresolved anomalies without coming down at all. The operators' choice is to cooperate or to watch.

The offer: resume operations, keep your staff, your licences, your key material, your command authority. We will rebuild your ground segment and we will begin before you answer.

Reconstruction starts in week three, unconditionally, at every site including those belonging to operators who have not responded — and it takes **five months**, not seven weeks, because the Works produce fourteen hundred tonnes a day rather than nine thousand and precision antenna assemblies are not something an asteroid becomes quickly. Three hundred and forty compounds is roughly eleven days of total output and most of a year of the fabrication capacity that can actually make them.

The acquirer never asks for a key and never obtains one. A **Reeve** takes a chair in each network operations centre and the company continues to operate its own constellation, with its own credentials, under its own licences, for a customer it cannot refuse.

Two mid-sized commercial operators and one national system refuse. Their rebuilt ground stations are handed over anyway, complete and working, and their constellations die on schedule regardless. The GEO birds drift out of their slots; the LEO fleet comes down over four years, visibly. That the acquirer let it happen is why the next eleven operators believe the terms.

Sixty-eight per cent of commercial capacity is back under acquirer tasking by month eight. The interdiction's cost, priced in advance and ordered anyway: approximately **2.4 million** dead of logistics failure over fourteen months.

**What comes with the estate is worse than the estate.** User terminals are individually addressable, and service can be permitted, degraded, or withdrawn per dish and per region. Handed to an occupier, the resistance's best communications option becomes revocable one antenna at a time, and connectivity becomes something a district is granted rather than something it has. By year three, with the seeker magazine gone, this is the most effective counter-insurgency instrument the acquirer possesses, and it is not a weapon.

**A category is never acquired.** National reconnaissance, signals intelligence, and military communications spacecraft run on closed networks from cleared facilities with their own key material, operated by people whose motivations are not commercial. Their ground stations are destroyed in the Wave and never rebuilt; the Limpets sit on the spacecraft; that is the end of it. For six years there are functioning reconnaissance satellites above an occupied planet, tasked by nobody, while their former ground crews keep reporting to a fenced field of rubble because no one told them to stop and because it is the only thing left that is theirs.

### Utility as Doctrine

**Wrights** — eighteen thousand, not forty-six — are on human infrastructure from day two to *maintain* it. When the grid strains in week three, they are at the substations with portable fusion cells. Nothing at any of the 438 reactor sites melts down in six years, and this remains the most consequential thing the operation achieves.

From week three they also build, and by year four the occupation has put up more than it destroyed: the ground segment, three hundred bridges, nine ports, and most of two national grids that were failing before any of this started. Destroy what is cheap to rebuild and then rebuild it, because both halves are cheap — Ground Rule 4 read forwards.

**Carters** move bulk, loud and slow by design. **Leeches** run medicine better than what preceded them, without distinguishing by nationality or by whose brother is in the resistance; a population that believes the occupier will treat its children does not have to be garrisoned. **Reeves** — a hundred and twenty thousand, which is far too few, and the administrative thinness of years two and three is the direct result — do the interface, unarmed and patient past any human tolerance.

**Gleaners** consume five hundred thousand destroyed vehicles and **Querns** turn the feedstock into Beadles, Scythes, Springes, and Lymers. By month five most of the occupation's ground inventory is manufactured out of the armies it defeated. What the Gleaners cannot produce is a single precision seeker, a beam director, or a compute core, and the gap between what the planet can supply and what the manifest carried is the shape of the entire second half of the campaign.

### Legitimacy

The occupation does not fabricate leaders, forge broadcasts, or lie — not from ethics but from demonstrated return. The Notification's accuracy bought more compliance in thirty hours than any threat could have; the rebuilt ground stations bought two-thirds of the world's satellite capacity; letting three constellations die untouched is what made the terms credible.

Insurgency is managed rather than defeated. Capture and release. No collective punishment, ever, on the explicit grounds that reprisal manufactures insurgents faster than it removes them. Bombers are arrested and imprisoned by human courts under human law, which continues to function.

The floor the acquirer cannot raise is the bottom of the envelope: a rifle at three metres, a buried charge with no electronics, a knife, a runner with a folded note, a tarpaulin. These need no network, no lock, no fuel, and no satellite, and after month fourteen the acquirer has no answer to the tarpaulin either.

## Phase 6: Consolidation

Charter tests:

1. No organised armed resistance above battalion scale. Met, **month 19**.
2. Global industrial output above the pre-arrival baseline. Met, **month 51**.
3. Civil administration functioning without acquirer staffing below the national level. Met, **month 62**.
4. Military commitment reduced to the orbital tier and a residual ground presence under two per cent of peak. Met, **month 71**.

**One month inside the schedule.**

**Final casualty accounting, six years:**

| Cause | Deaths |
|---|---|
| The Wave (30 hours) | ~340,000 |
| Boost-phase wreckage and contamination, four footprints | ~180,000 |
| Regional nuclear use, month 3 | ~200,000 |
| Conventional campaign and hunt, military | ~5,600,000 |
| Conventional campaign and hunt, civilian | ~3,100,000 |
| Logistics, medical, and financial disruption, year 1–2 — *of which ~2,400,000 attributable to the ground-segment destruction* | ~13,400,000 |
| State collapse, civil conflict, and localised famine, years 1–4 | ~33,000,000 |
| Insurgency and enforcement, years 1–6 | ~6,200,000 |
| **Total** | **~62,000,000 (0.78%)** |

**Ninety per cent of everyone who dies in these six years dies of logistics.** The single largest thing keeping the number at sixty-two million instead of five hundred million is that the grid never went down. The single largest thing keeping it at sixty-two million instead of six is that orbit went dark for eight months and the acquirer knew roughly what that would cost and did it anyway.

The plan budgeted four hundred million and came in at fifteen per cent of budget, and the Seneschal does not record this as an achievement, because the margin was never in its control.

## Epilogue: Year 6

The lights are on because they were never turned off.

The follow-on flow arrives on schedule and finds a working planet, one month inside the charter: ports operating, rail running, the grid carrying more load than before because half of it is now fusion-supplemented, seven billion nine hundred million people going to work, resenting it, and going anyway. Twenty-three national governments still hold elections; the results are not interfered with, because the acquirer does not care who wins a question it has already answered.

There are eleven insurgencies and there will be for a hundred years. Somewhere in Anatolia there is an aircraft under a tarpaulin that has been rebuilt twice from three others and has not flown since month fifty-one, and the acquirer does not know where it is, and will not, because the last precision seeker was expended thirty-four months ago against a heated dummy in a Carpathian forest.

There is a teleport outside Luxembourg that is four years old and better than the one it replaced, and inside it the same shift that was on duty on the first day is on duty now. They command their own spacecraft, with their own keys, under their own licences, and a Reeve sits in the corner and has sat there for five years, and it has never once given an order that was not phrased as a request.

The Bounds have not moved in six years. Nobody looks up at them any more, which is what they were for.

In a school outside Chicago a girl who was four when it happened is taught the six years as history and finds it hard to follow, because nothing in the account explains the part she wants explained, which is why. Her teacher does not know either. Nobody was ever told. The transmission on the first day said what would happen and was accurate about every detail — the times, the regions, the order, and the promise to rebuild — and it did not say what for, and in six years of scrupulous public honesty the acquirer has never once volunteered the reason, not because it is a secret but because nobody asked it in a form it recognised as a question requiring an answer.

The answer, had anyone found the right form, is that Earth is on the way to somewhere else.

## Appendix: What Changed and Why

1. **The industrial bootstrap was the largest violation of the one-century rule and it is now split in two.** Bulk mass from an asteroid is credible. A self-reproducing ecosystem of semiconductor fabrication, optical figuring, and parts-per-billion separation, standing up in nineteen months, is not. Everything whose difficulty is miniaturisation is shipped, once, and cannot be replaced inside the campaign.

2. **Scarcity is the best thing that happened to this plan.** In every earlier version the acquirer never had to choose. The manifest makes redundancy cost something, makes losses permanent, and gives the campaign a shape: a lavish opening it can afford and a long middle it cannot.

3. **The seeker magazine is the operation's real clock.** Correlation seekers are cheap and sufficient against targets that agreed to be where the survey found them. Precision seekers are irreplaceable and are the only thing that finds what hides. They run out in month fourteen and the last five years are fought without them.

4. **The Fowler's beam justification was a copy-paste error and it deserved to be caught.** Ninety-eight per cent of the atmosphere being below the platform helps enormously against something climbing into thin air and not at all against something on a runway at the bottom of the whole column. The Hayward's case survives; the Fowler's does not. The beam is demoted to clear-air engagement and sensor-blinding — which needs orders of magnitude less energy and works through atmosphere that defeats a structural kill — and runway suppression passes to the Springe.

5. **Humans get a win and keep it.** Three air forces establish that sorties into weather survive, and the acquirer never closes the hole; it waits four years for the spares to run out. This is the first version of the plan in which the adversary discovers a real limitation and exploits it systematically.

6. **The Domesday is a map, not a blanket.** Four hundred billion gram-scale elements seeded against road networks, installations, chokepoints, and dispersal terrain — two per cent of land at high density, nine at useful density, nothing at all across the rest. The edges are published by the resistance in month eight.

7. **The Tithing is better than what it replaces and costs no hardware.** A billion civilian cameras, forty million traffic sensors, six hundred million connected vehicles, on networks the acquirer already owns. Worthless where the Domesday is best and extraordinary where it fails. The occupation watches the planet through the apparatus the planet built to watch itself.

8. **The Lampreys were the least defensible claim in the plan.** Four of fourteen, not eleven. The hunt runs seven months, and one boat is never found at all — it surfaces in month nine because the crew ran out of food.

9. **The Hayward has a thermal magazine.** Forty seconds of cumulative beam-on, then ninety minutes. Irrelevant within one boost window, absolute across two, and the two-wave salvo that would have defeated it is available at no cost to an adversary that never discovers it. It sits at the top of the unexploited column for all six years.

10. **Everything is slower and the schedule now bites.** Organised resistance to month 19, ground segment rebuilt in five months, charter met in month 71 with one month of margin instead of four.

11. **Still thin.** Eleven thousand objects in low orbit over eight weeks remains unconcealable and unsolved — now unambiguously the largest hole in the plan. Compact fusion at 2.4 tonnes per core is generous. The Tithing's fusion of a billion heterogeneous civilian feeds is a compute claim, not a sensing claim, and compute is the thing the manifest is shortest of. And the whole venture is still one man in the Atlantic away from being a completely different document — for two hundred and eighty-one days, now, instead of twenty-two.
